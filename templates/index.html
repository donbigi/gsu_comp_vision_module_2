Why the measurements are ~cm-accurate

A pinhole camera maps a 3-D point to pixels as x = fx·(X/Z) + cx. For a planar object facing the camera, inverting that equation gives a real length spanning dx pixels as

L = (dx / fx) · Z

so a length needs only the focal length fx (from calibration) and the measured camera-to-object distance Z. The principal point cancels when taking pixel differences. This page runs the whole pipeline — chessboard calibration, white-paper detection, bar measurement — and compares the result to 20 ground-truth lengths.
